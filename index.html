Renewal Review Workbench

A Claude artifact on live CRM and CS-platform data, read-only
Problem

Monthly renewal reviews ran on a static spreadsheet. Meetings were spent clicking between systems, and decisions had nowhere to live between reviews.

Built

A Claude artifact that puts the CSM's notes, the renewal rep's notes, value at risk, forecast, and health on one card per renewal. Managers send Slack pre-reads and log decisions to a shared record.

Result
  • 100% of renewal reviews across Enterprise, international, and Mid-Market; spreadsheet retired
  • 85% of $250K+ renewals reviewed 90+ days before close
  • 25% less late-identified risk and 10% better forecast accuracy on reviewed renewals
  • Load time cut from 5 minutes to 30 seconds

Next90% early-review coverage and 40% less late-identified risk by April 2027.

Account-Assignment Workbench

One place to assign every CSM, with capacity and fit checked before anything moves
Problem

CSM assignments ran in spreadsheets and were executed one account at a time. Nothing checked whether the receiving CSM had room, whether the account fit their segment, or whether a customer family was being split.

Built

A workbench for every kind of assignment: handoffs straight from Implementation, leave coverage, new-hire books, departures, and rebalances. It ranks candidates on capacity (account count and ARR as separate limits), sales-pod fit, complexity, and region. The manager approves and the move ships as one batch.

Result
  • 100% of CSM assignments in Enterprise and Mid-Market; 800+ moves
  • Median cycle of 3 business days
  • 90% of books within capacity; ownership errors down 80%
  • Validated ~2 hours per manager per week, ~700 hours a year in Enterprise

Next1,500 moves, a two-day cycle, and 95% of books within capacity by April 2027.

Enterprise KPI Command Center

CS Carve Studio

The annual book-of-business carve, run on agreed rules instead of by hand
Problem

The prior year's carve data arrived late and hurt the start of the year. Books were balanced by hand, with no shared rules and no way to compare scenarios.

Built

Each territory carries its agreed rules: caps, disruption limits, renewal locks, and sales-pod alignment. Auto-balance moves whole customer families into the best-fit book, and nothing saves until a person confirms. It surfaced two conflicts before sign-off that would otherwise have landed late.

Result
  • Books signed off 30+ days earlier than the prior year
  • August 15 go-live hit with every book inside caps or under a signed exception
  • Moves within 5% of plan; under 3% unplanned re-carve

NextRun the FY29 carve in the tool, with under 5% cumulative re-carve.

Renewal-Driver Analysis

An agentic Claude workflow across CRM, warehouse, CS platform, and enterprise search
Problem

The team tracked about eight activities it assumed drove renewal, and its renewal metric didn't match Finance's certified rate.

Built

One workflow that pulled two years of renewals from four systems, ran a size-controlled logistic regression, and produced a director-ready report.

Result
  • Only 2 of ~8 assumed drivers stay predictive once size, segment, and tenure are controlled for
  • Renewal metric reconciled to the certified Finance rate
  • Findings used to design the KPIs the command center now tracks, and fed FY27 planning

Principles

How I work

Build it, prove it, hand it off

The goal is other teams' operating process, not a tool I maintain. Success is when the tool stops being the point.

Make the trusted path the easy one

If the governed number is harder to get than a side spreadsheet, people use the spreadsheet. I build so the right answer is also the fastest one.

Ground it or don't claim it

Findings are predictive, not causal, and reconciled to the certified number. Savings are claimed only after users confirm the old process stopped.

Let's talk.

I'm best on problems where nobody has agreed what the number means yet, and the fix is as much a decision about ownership as it is a build.
